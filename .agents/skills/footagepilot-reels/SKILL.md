# FootagePilot reels

Follow the stage the user asks for. For new footage, develop the concept and shooting brief first. When the user already has an album, inventory it and choose a concept the footage can carry. Read the [inspiration guide](references/inspiration.md) for research and the [tool guide](references/tools.md) for intake, editing, and delivery.

## Develop the concept

- Establish the subject, audience, occasion, and any people or moments that must appear. Use the user's saved references for taste and current public examples for format or music research.
- Watch the examples through. Identify the opening promise, progression, and payoff. Inspect the frames and listen around the cuts you want to adapt. For each borrowed technique, identify the footage or sound it depends on.
- If asked for options, give two or three concepts with genuinely different stories or visual structures. For each, state what the viewer sees first, what changes, what earns the ending, and what is feasible to shoot. Cite public examples. Keep private reference details in the local project.
- When the user asks to see the idea first, deliver the shooting brief and end that stage there. When the user has supplied a usable brief and footage, proceed directly to editing.

## Give the user a shooting brief

Start with a one-sentence concept, the approximate runtime and aspect ratio, and the sound plan. Fill one row per planned story beat. Use the same IDs at intake.

| Shot ID and role | What to film or photograph | How to capture it | Cut and audio cue | Priority or fallback |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| S01 · hook | Subject steps through a dark doorway toward camera | Vertical medium, camera still. Record through the step with a little room before and after. | Cut as the shoulder covers the lens. Keep the first footstep. | Required. Use a passing pillar as the wipe if the shoulder move is impractical. |

Specify the subject, action, framing, camera position, movement, lighting, and usable duration wherever they affect the edit. For a match cut, describe both sides and their shared position, direction, or pose. For live sound, identify what to record and where it belongs. Mark stills explicitly when they serve a role such as a portrait, collage panel, or closing hold. Prioritize the shots that carry the story so the user can finish a shorter shoot. Include a repeatable movement or backup take for fragile transitions. See the [capture-first examples](../../../docs/REFERENCE-EDITORIAL.md).

## Receive and edit

1. Ask for the Google Photos album name or link, or a local folder, when the media is ready. Receive the authorized scope, preserve the originals, and map available takes to the brief. Check required people and activities against the inventory when the brief calls for broad event coverage. Resolve a missing shot with a supported story beat, a pickup, or a revised concept. Record the decision.
2. Build a storyboard or timeline with a reason for each shot. Choose transitions that follow motion, sound, composition, or a change in scene. Match exposure and white balance across shots before applying a LUT or other look. Set timing from the actual soundtrack and recorded sound.
3. Choose the editor by the effect and deliverable. Keep an editable project and a verified export when supported. If variants are requested, vary the narrative or visual structure and record what changed.
4. Decode the entire export, check the opening, key cuts, payoff, crop and subject visibility, title readability, and sound. Watch and listen through where the tools allow it. Record technical results and aesthetic feedback independently. Deliver the local review export or share through the channel authorized for the task.

Keep captures, reference media, private collection links, download receipts, projects, and renders under ignored `projects/` or another approved local path. Preserve reusable presets, sanitized findings, and source documentation in the repository when requested.
